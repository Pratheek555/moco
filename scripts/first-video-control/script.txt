The Leaning Tower of Pisa is still standing because its builders went to war.
In eleven seventy-eight, just a few floors up, the tower began to tip.
The ground beneath was soft, wet clay, like building on a sponge.
Then Pisa went to war, and the builders packed up and left.
For almost a century, the half-built tower sat there, leaning.
Pigeons raised great-grandchildren on it. Locals called it a ruin.
But you see, all that waiting let the soil squeeze down and harden.
Had they kept building, it would have fallen.
The war that stopped the tower is the reason it still stands.
