## Phase 7: premium scene, feature costs and render budget

**Rows.** All rows use 300 frames (10 s), H.264, JPEG frames and concurrency 8, with the median of 3 runs. Preview is scale 0.5 (540×960); final is 1080×1920.
- `baseline`: the Phase 0 scene as it parses today. The 0.2.0 spec defaults turn on idle motion, vignette and grain, so it no longer looks like Phase 0.
- `baseline-phase0-look`: the same scene with those three turned off. That is the old look on the new layered-SVG Krishna.
- `premium`: `bench/scenes/premium-krishna-dawn.json`, the golden scene.
- `premium-no-<feature>`: the premium scene with one feature switched off (`bench/variants.ts`).
- The Rive rows are covered in the Phase 5 note above.

**Machine:** Apple M2 Max (12 cores), 32 GB RAM, macOS 27.2. Remotion 4.0.539 with its Chrome Headless Shell, `gl` default. **GPU mode is unknown:** `npx remotion gpu` fails with `ERR_FAILED` (see the GPU note above). Treat these as CPU-composited numbers.

### Feature cost

How much faster the premium render gets with each feature switched off, as a share of the premium time:

| feature | final: premium 7.26 s | preview: premium 3.81 s |
|---|---|---|
| grain | **23.6%** (−1.71 s) | 9.4% |
| atmosphere (dust + light rays) | **10.3%** (−0.75 s) | 11.0% |
| vignette | **9.5%** (−0.67 s) | 7.1% |
| grade (soft-light) | 7.0% (−0.50 s) | 11.0% |
| lip sync (viseme mouth) | 2.1% | 2.4% |
| idle motion | 0.4% | 5.5% |
| parallax | −0.4% | 2.4% |
| **premium vs `baseline-phase0-look`** | **+53%** (7.26 s vs 4.75 s) | +38% (3.81 s vs 2.75 s) |

**Noise.** Runs within a session vary by 3–6%. The same variant measured an hour apart drifted by up to 13% (`baseline-phase0-look` final: 4.19 s, then 4.75 s). Anything under about 5% (lip sync, idle, parallax) can't be told apart from zero. The rig, lip sync and parallax are effectively free. **The cost is in the full-frame overlays:** grain, vignette, grade and light rays each paint over all 2 million pixels on every frame.

**Already fixed in this phase (grain).** The first matrix put grain at 38% of a 9.52 s premium render. The grain `<Img>` swapped its `src` every 2 frames, and Remotion's `<Img>` loads and decodes the image again on each swap. Now all 3 grain images stay mounted and only their opacity changes (`renderer/src/remotion/Look.tsx`). Results: **premium final 9.52 s → 7.26 s (−24%), preview 4.66 s → 3.81 s (−18%)**. Golden stills and the byte-exact determinism test still pass.

### Slowest frames and why

Final quality: frames 0–7 take 310–470 ms each, frames 8–9 take 125–170 ms, and steady state is about 20 ms per frame. With concurrency 8, frames 0–7 are each browser tab's first frame. That frame pays for opening the page, loading and decoding every `<Img>` (sky, tint, grade, vignette, 3 grain images, dust, rays), and laying out the caption font. It is a fixed cost of about 0.5 s per tab, paid once per render whatever its length. A 45 s reel spreads it over 1,350 frames.

### Optimisations for the top 3 remaining costs (not yet measured)

1. **Grain (23.6%).** Move it out of the browser into the encoder. `renderMedia({ ffmpegOverride })` exists in 4.0.539 (checked in `ffmpeg-override.d.ts`; hooks `pre-stitcher` and `stitcher`), and it can add FFmpeg's `noise=alls=…:allf=t` with a fixed seed. That removes a full-frame layer from every frame, so I'd expect most of the 1.7 s back. Before adopting it, check that FFmpeg's seeded noise gives identical bytes on every run (the determinism rule). A cheaper step: bake the vignette into the 3 grain images. Both use normal blending, so two full-frame layers become one.
2. **Atmosphere (10.3%).** The light rays are a full-frame image rotated by up to 1.5° on every frame, which forces a full resample. Keep the gentle opacity "breathing", drop the rotation (or bake 2–3 slightly rotated frames and cross-fade them), and draw the 18 dust motes from one sprite sheet. Expected gain is about half of the 10%.
3. **Vignette (9.5%)**, which also bears on the grade (7%). Merge the normal-blend overlays: vignette into grain (as in 1), or vignette into the time-of-day tint, since both are static. The soft-light grade can't merge with normal-blend layers, but it can be skipped at preview quality, where colour accuracy matters less.

### The 45 s reel number and a proposed budget

**Measured on this machine (M2 Max, 12 cores, concurrency 8): the premium look costs 24.2 ms per frame at final quality, so a 45 s reel (1,350 frames) takes about 33 s. Preview takes about 17 s.** These numbers are extrapolated from the 10 s scene's cost per frame, and they include H.264 encoding. Muxing the voiceover adds well under a second.

Caveats:
- The engine's render port defaults to concurrency 2, because it shares the machine with Claude calls. The concurrency sweep above puts concurrency 2 at about 1.9× slower than 8, so in the app today a final 45 s reel takes **about 60 s** on this machine.
- A mid-range laptop (8 cores, for example an M1/M2 Air or a Ryzen 5 / Core i5 with 16 GB) should be about 2–3× slower than an M2 Max. That's an estimate, not a measurement.

**Proposed budget, for you to approve:**
- **Final 45 s reel: ≤ 2 min on a mid-range laptop.** The equivalent here is ≤ 45 s; we measure 33 s at concurrency 8 and about 60 s at the app's concurrency 2.
- **Preview 45 s reel: ≤ 45 s on a mid-range laptop** (≤ 20 s here; we measure 17 s).
- To meet it at the app's settings: use concurrency min(cores − 2, 6) once AI calls for the episode are finished, and do optimisation 1. Then re-measure on a real mid-range machine before launch.
