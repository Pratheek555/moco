# How Horseflies Bite You 😨

Semi-real 3D macro anatomy explainer: second-person run-on narration, constant camera, snap-move transitions (3d_cartoon) · Zack D. Films · <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6mRR91AbxjQ>

A clean, glossy, semi-realistic 3D render treats the body as terrain. The camera works at ground-level macro, or slices into cutaways, and every mechanism in the script gets its own literal close-up. A deadpan second-person voice runs almost without a break (196 wpm while speaking, 89% coverage). The camera moves in 85% of frames, and shots are joined by 1–9 frame continuous camera moves rather than hard cuts. Pacing switches between bursts of 1-word inserts and long single-move explanation shots. Props stay dead still except for 3–6 frame mechanical action bursts led by their verbs, and the last wide shot matches the first so the video loops.

## format

- Make a 9:16 rendered 3D short of about 25–30 s. Use no title card and no outro. Narration starts on frame 1 and the video stops on the last action word. **27.6 s, 1080x1920, 18 shots** _(high; Measured duration; transcript runs 0–27.04 s)_

## render

- Render a clean semi-realistic 3D look with no outlines and no toon ramps. Give it strong specular highlights, fine surface bump, glossy wet materials and a soft background depth of field. Add no grain. _(high; S3–S8 chitin and blades, S12 metal teeth, S13/S17 glossy blood, S1/S18 skin bump)_

## sets

- Make each exterior set one curved surface that falls away like a horizon, under a flat bright gradient sky, with zero other props. Treat the body part as a landscape. **sky covers 21–42% of the frame in exterior shots** _(high; S1, S3–S9, S14, S18)_
- For anything that happens inside, cut to a cross-section slice that shows stacked tissue layers. The slice should fill the frame. _(high; S10–S13, S15, S17)_

## colour

- Exterior shots: set warm peach surface tones against a complementary cyan sky. Interior shots: switch to a saturated all-red-and-pink palette. Give the active tool a gold or metal colour so it stands out against both. **skin #b97353–#ea9871 30–47%; sky #45afcc–#6eb3c9; interior #e76472 / #98232b; tool #f2c16e** _(high; S1–S9 vs S10–S13, S15, S17)_
- Mark a consequence by tinting the subject to a hot emissive colour over 3 frames, 0–15 frames after the key word. Hold the glow while the subject keeps creeping slowly. **3 frames** _(high; M10: 'burst' at f481, glow f494–496)_

## lighting

- Use a bright soft key from the top or front plus sky fill. Keep contrast moderate and highlights crisp. Never let the subject go dark or moody. _(medium; All shots)_

## characters

- Make the subject a single detailed semi-real model and reuse it in every shot. If you need a human, use one neutral semi-realistic stand-in for 'you', once, for scale only. **human appears in 1 of 18 shots** _(high; S2; subject in S1, S3–S8, S14, S18)_

## faces

- Use no faces, blinks or lip sync. The faceless object is the actor. Put the drama into what it does and into material and colour changes. **0 faces across all burst sheets** _(high; M3, M5, M6, M10, M12, M16 sheets)_

## gestures

- Animate each tool action in 4–6 frames with linear or ease-in motion. Use no anticipation and no overshoot, and hand off straight into a camera move instead of a settle. **4–6 frames** _(high; M3 slice f223–228, camera dive takes over at f229)_
- For a repeated action (saw, pump, pulse), alternate two parts in opposite phase. Each stroke is 3–5% of frame height and lasts 3–4 frames. Start the loop 10–12 frames before the verb that names it. **3–5% of frame height per 3–4 frames; lead 10–12 frames** _(medium; M6 motion f360–366, 'sawing' at f372 (stroke size estimated by eye))_
- Start each illustrative action 1–5 frames before its word's first frame. Place return or aftermath beats 6–13 frames after the word. **leads 1–5 frames; lags 6–13 frames** _(medium; Leads of 2, 1 and 5 frames (slice, like, laps); lags of 6 and 13 frames (saw return, burst glow))_

## acting

- Between beats, keep props completely still: no idle drift, no breathing. Holds last 4–20 frames. All continuity comes from the moving camera. **0.008–0.01 px/frame of leftover motion** _(high; M5 f337–355 hold, M10 f497–504)_

## facts to pictures

- Give each mechanism in the script its own extreme close-up of the part doing it. When the script counts parts, fan them out and show each one. _(high; S4–S9 count reveal; S10–S13, S15, S17)_
- Draw comparisons literally. Morph the part's material into the compared object with a 3-frame in-shot dissolve that starts 0–2 frames before the comparison word. Hold it 15–20 frames, then dissolve back over 3 frames once the noun has been spoken. **3-frame dissolve, hold 15–20 frames** _(high; M5/M6: dissolve f334–336 (>like at f335), hold f337–355, back f356–358)_
- When the narration names a hidden internal process, make the outer surface translucent or drain it away over 2–3 frames, 3–5 frames before the verb. Then animate the inside on the verb. **2–3 frame reveal, 3–5 frames before the verb** _(high; M16: drain f778–780, translucent at f781, >laps at f783)_

## framing

- Shoot at a low, surface-level macro angle looking up, so the key part fills most of the frame. Keep the hero on the centre vertical, within about 10% of width. _(high; S3–S9; T1, T9, T13 sheets)_
- Bookend the video with a centred wide shot of the subject small on its surface. Across a transition, change scale by 3–5×. **3–5× scale change** _(medium; S1/S18; T1 subject goes from ~40% to ~8% of frame)_
- Keep the lower 30% of the frame low in detail so the caption reads cleanly. _(medium; Caption band at about 75–80% height in all shots)_

## camera

- Give each shot exactly one move on one main axis (push-in, tilt down or tilt up) with no reversal. Cruise at 25–65%/s in shots over 2 s and creep at 5–8%/s in inserts under 0.5 s. **moving in 85% of frames; mid speeds 25–65%/s; inserts 5–8%/s** _(high; S9 zoom +172% in 3.7 s; S12 tilt -132% at 26%/s mid; S13 tilt +124% at 65%/s mid; S5–S7 at 4.8–7.9%/s)_
- Cross from outside to inside the body with one long continuous push or tilt through the surface. Leave the inside later with the reverse move. _(high; S9 push-in, T9 dive into the cross-section, T13 rise back out)_
- Arrive moving: enter each shot at 4–28× its cruise speed and ease down to cruise over 6–10 frames. If the shot hands off with a move, ramp up to 1.5–2.5× cruise over the last 10–15 frames. **entry 4–28× cruise; exit 1.5–2.5× cruise** _(high; S2 304.6/10.8/292.5 %/s; S14 329/66.6/259.8; S13 9.8/64.7/158.5)_
- Allow one dead-still hold of about 4 s on an explanatory beat, then leave it by easing up to about 50%/s over its last 10 frames. **~4 s at 0.1%/s, exit at ~53%/s** _(high; S15 at 0.1/0.1/53 %/s, 34% of frames moving)_
- Reveal by tilting at a steady speed across a textured surface. Bring the next subject in over the frame edge 8–10 frames before the transition and keep accelerating into it. **62 → 169 %/s** _(medium; S11: top edge enters at f274, head revealed by f281)_
- End the final shot with a short zoom-out that decelerates to a full stop on the last word. **zoom -14.7%, 148 → 0.2 %/s** _(high; S18)_

## transitions

- Join shots with fast continuous camera moves through the same 3D space, with directional motion blur on the in-between frames. Lengths: snap moves 1–2 frames, zoom-throughs 5–7, whips 3–9 (default 5). Never use cross-dissolves between shots. **17 transitions: 7 snap, 4 whip, 3 zoom, 3 blur** _(high; T2 f45–46, T9 f229–231, T13 f524–526)_
- Pair your moves: an orbit-out is answered by an orbit-back, and a dive-in by a pull-out. Carry momentum into the next shot's first frames. **out speed ≥ in speed in 10 of 15 transitions** _(medium; T1/T2 orbit pair 0.57 s apart; T9/T13 dive and rise)_
- Don't snap transitions to word or clause onsets. Place each change 4–12 frames into the word it illustrates, mid-sentence, while the voice keeps running. **35% within ±2 frames of a word onset (chance 38%); 6% within ±4 frames of a clause start (chance 8%)** _(high; Sync stats; the current word started a median of about 7 frames before each cut)_

## pace

- Mix two speeds: a burst of 4–6 inserts at 6–12 frames each (about 1 word per shot) on the line that names or counts the parts, then long single-move shots of 100–145 frames carrying 10–14 words each. **median shot 0.82 s, a new picture every 1.53 s; p10–p90 0.31–3.89 s** _(high; S4–S8 at 0.2–0.4 s each; S9 3.7 s, S12 4.84 s, S15 4.34 s)_

## hook

- Open on 'When [subject] does [action] to you,' plus a concrete count, over the subject alone in shot 1. Cut to the human victim on the word 'you' within 1 s. **first cut at 0.93 s; hook is 14 words / 5.12 s** _(high; S1, S2)_

## script

- Write 3 sentences of about 15 / 40 / 25 words: a hook with a count; a long chained escalation ending in a bodily result; then a 'Next,' sentence that finishes the process. End on the final physical action, with no moral and no call to action. **14 / 40 / 26 words, 80 words total** _(high; Sentences at 0–5.12 s, 5.46–17.86 s, 18.18–27.04 s)_
- Make the viewer the object of every action, in second person. Chain steps with so / while / until / which / as. Use one blunt physical verb per step, and pair each unfamiliar part with a household tool it works like. Pay off the hook's count item by item. **6 second-person words out of 80** _(high; Transcript)_

## voice

- Use one deadpan narrator, fast and unbroken. Leave no gaps between words. Pause about 10 frames after sentences and 6–12 frames after commas. Keep the level flat and let pitch carry the emphasis. **196 wpm speaking, 174 wpm wall to wall, 89% coverage; pauses 0.33 s (sentence) and 0.21–0.4 s (clause)** _(high; Pause and pitch stats; median pitch 176 Hz)_

## music

- Run a constant quiet music bed about 15 dB under the voice, with no swells and no obvious ducking. **voice -14.6 dB, pauses -29.3 dB** _(medium; Audio numbers)_

## sound

- Master loud and compressed, around -10 to -11 LUFS with a narrow loudness range, but keep true peak below -1 dBFS (the reference clips). Optional: whooshes on whips and wet hits on fluid beats, kept under the voice. **-10.5 LUFS, LRA 2.3 LU** _(low; Loudness numbers; sound effects inferred from the picture only)_

## captions

- Set a single line of small white bold sans with a soft dark drop shadow, no box and no outline, centred at about 75–80% of frame height. Keep it in the same position and colour all video. A 4-word line covers no more than about 70% of the frame width. **baseline about 78% of height; letters about 3% of frame height** _(high; S1–S18 tiles; 'bites you' ≈32% of width, 4-word line ≈69%)_
- Show 2–4 word key phrases with punctuation and hyphens removed and no highlight words. Hard-swap them within 0–3 frames of the phrase's first word, with no animation. Hold a phrase across bursts of fast cuts. **2–4 words per chunk; swap within 0–3 frames** _(high; 4-word chunk held across S4–S8 (~1.8 s, 5 shots); S12 swap at f306, word starts at f303)_

## graphics

- Use no labels, arrows, icons or diagram overlays. The render shows every fact, and the caption is the only overlay. _(high; All 18 overview tiles)_

## loop

- End on a wide shot that repeats the opening composition (subject on the surface, same sky) so the replay looks continuous, even if the words don't connect. **last-to-first frame similarity 0.74** _(high; S1 vs S18 palettes and composition)_

## Never

- handheld or simulated camera shake
- hard locked-off frames, apart from the one ~4 s explanatory hold
- cross-dissolves between shots
- reversing camera direction within a shot
- toon outlines, cel shading or film grain
- faces, expressions or lip sync on the subject
- idle drift or breathing on props between action beats
- anticipation or overshoot on tool actions
- labels, arrows, icons or diagram overlays
- caption boxes, word highlights, pop or fade caption animation, or changing caption colour
- cluttered sets or extra props
- dark, moody or low-key lighting
- snapping cuts to word or sentence onsets
- title cards, outros, morals or calls to action
- dramatic music swells
- true peak above 0 dBFS
