# Cartoon fact shorts

A short of about 29 s with a clean, semi-real 3D render. Despite the name, there are no toon outlines and no cel shading. One deadpan narrator talks almost non-stop: 196 wpm while speaking, with speech over 88% of the runtime. The script is one unbroken chain of causes, opens on the danger or action already happening, and ends on the final outcome with no outro. The world stays frozen like a diorama. Props and people hold dead still apart from short 4-frame action bursts. All the life comes from the camera, which moves in 88% of frames, makes one move on one axis per shot, arrives fast and eases to a cruise. Shots are joined mostly by roughly 6-frame blur, zoom-through or whip transitions, with a new picture about every 1.9 s. Each fact becomes a literal picture: a close-up of the part at work, a cutaway, a display inside the scene, or a morph into the thing it is compared to. That picture appears on the word that names it. There are no overlays. The only text is a small white caption of 2–4 words at 78% of frame height. The last shot leans back toward the first so the video loops.

> Learned by moco on 2026-11-03 from 2 reference videos, measured frame by frame.
> Numbers and how they were measured: [references/measurements.md](references/measurements.md).
> Technique only: the references' characters, sets, signs, topics and lines are not part of this style.

## Format & pace

A short, dense, wall-to-wall piece with two shot speeds.

### Length
- **Default:** About 29 s (29.3 s), 9:16, 1080x1920.
- **Range:** 27.6–31.1 s
- **Deviate when:** A five-beat story can run to about 31 s. A three-sentence process can stop at about 27.5 s. Never pad it.

*Why:* Short enough to watch again, long enough for one full chain of causes.

### No bookends
- **Default:** No title card, no intro and no outro. Narration starts on frame 1. Picture and sound end within about 0.8 s of the last word, with the audio fading on that word.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** never

*Why:* The viewer is inside the story from the first frame, and the end flows straight into the replay.

### Picture rate
- **Default:** Show a new picture every 1.9 s (median 1.88 s).
- **Range:** Median 1.5–2.2 s across references. Within a video, p10–p90 runs 0.31–3.89 s.
- **Deviate when:** Use bursts of very short shots on lines that list or count things (see Two speeds). Give a result or an explanation shot 3–4.8 s.

*Why:* Something new arrives about every 4 words, so attention never drops.

### Two speeds
- **Default:** Alternate quick shots and long shots. On a listing line, cut a burst of 4–6 inserts at 6–12 frames each, about 1 word per shot. Give the decisive moment 30–48 frames. Carry explanations and results in long single-move shots of 90–145 frames.
- **Range:** One reference leans on 1-word insert bursts. The other keeps shots at 1–3.7 s.
- **Deviate when:** With no list or count in the script, skip the burst and use 1–1.6 s shots for the decisive beat.

*Why:* The change of rhythm marks what matters and stops the short feeling flat.

### Words per picture
- **Default:** About 4 spoken words per shot (median 4.25).
- **Range:** 2–6.5 across references. Within a video, 1–12 words.
- **Deviate when:** Inserts carry 1 word. Long explanation shots carry 10–14.

*Why:* Ties every picture to a phrase the viewer is hearing right then.

### First change
- **Default:** Make the first picture change at about 1.7 s, on the word that names who or what is affected.
- **Range:** 0.9–2.5 s
- **Deviate when:** If the affected party is named early in the hook, cut on that word even when it comes before 1 s.

*Why:* An early change confirms the video is moving and pays off the hook's subject.

## Script & hook

One deadpan chain of causes, with no breath between steps.

### Hook
- **Default:** Make the first sentence 11–14 words, ending by about 4.7 s. It names the threat or action as it happens, plus a concrete count (how many parts, how many people). Put the subject and its setting in the first frame.
- **Range:** Hook ends at 4.3–5.1 s
- **Deviate when:** never open with a question or 'did you know'

*Why:* Danger plus a number gives the viewer a reason to stay before any context.

### Shape
- **Default:** Write 3–5 sentences, about 80–90 words in total. Make the second sentence the longest: the setup or escalation, spread over the most shots. Hold back the answer to the hook until the last sentence.
- **Range:** 3 sentences of 14/40/26 words, or 5 sentences of 11/28/16/19/14 words
- **Deviate when:** For a step-by-step process, use 3 sentences: hook, long escalation, then a 'Next,' sentence that finishes it. For an event with a twist, use 5 beats: threat, a small unrelated action, what it caused, the strike, the payoff.

*Why:* A long middle builds momentum, and the late payoff keeps people watching to the end.

### Chaining
- **Default:** Start sentences with link words that show cause or time (so, while, until, which, as, next, just then). Chain two results per sentence with -ing clauses. Use one blunt physical verb per step.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** Save 'But' for the twist and the payoff only.

*Why:* Each step pulls the next one in, so there is never a natural place to swipe away.

### Voice of the script
- **Default:** Plain, deadpan statements of fact. No opinion words, no jokes.
- **Range:** Second person present ('you' as the object of every action) in one reference. Third person past tense in the other.
- **Deviate when:** Use second person when the process happens to the viewer's own body or life. Use third person past tense for a true event.

*Why:* The flat delivery lets the facts carry the shock.

### Ending
- **Default:** End on the final physical action or the stated outcome. No moral, no summary, no call to action.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** never

*Why:* Stopping on the result makes the replay feel like part of the story.

### Loop
- **Default:** Bring the last shot back toward the opening composition (same subject placement, same backdrop), or end the last sentence on the word that ended the first.
- **Range:** Last-to-first frame correlation 0.61 (0.49–0.74)
- **Deviate when:** If the story ends somewhere else, keep at least the matching last word.

*Why:* A seamless replay counts as more watch time.

## Voice & sound

A fast, flat narrator over a quiet, steady bed, mastered loud.

### Speaking rate
- **Default:** 196 wpm while speaking, about 172 wpm wall to wall, with speech over about 88% of the short. No gaps between words inside a clause.
- **Range:** 196.3–196.4 wpm speaking; 170–174 wpm overall; coverage 86.6–88.7%
- **Deviate when:** never slower

*Why:* The speed itself creates urgency.

### Pauses
- **Default:** About 0.37 s after a sentence and 0.34 s after a clause.
- **Range:** Sentence 0.3–0.4 s (up to 0.55 s within a video); clause 0.3–0.4 s
- **Deviate when:** Before the payoff sentence, allow one longer pause of about 0.6 s.

*Why:* Tiny breaths keep it readable without breaking the momentum.

### Delivery
- **Default:** One deadpan, news-like narrator. Median pitch about 171 Hz with a range of about 16 semitones. Keep the level flat and use pitch for emphasis: lift it on the hook, the twist word and the impact verb.
- **Range:** Pitch 165–176 Hz; range 14.6–16.8 semitones (the higher figure may be inflated by music)
- **Deviate when:** never add character voices or acting

*Why:* Calm delivery of shocking facts is the style's tone.

### Music bed
- **Default:** One steady music bed about 12.7 dB under the voice. No swells, no ducking. Fade it on the last word.
- **Range:** 10.6–14.7 dB under the voice
- **Deviate when:** never add a drop or swell

*Why:* The bed adds pace without competing with the words.

### Master
- **Default:** About -10 LUFS integrated, with a narrow loudness range (about 2 LU). Keep true peak at or below -1 dBFS.
- **Range:** -10.5 to -9.7 LUFS. Both references clip; don't copy that.
- **Deviate when:** never exceed -1 dBFS true peak

*Why:* Loud and even on phone speakers, without distortion.

### Sound effects
- **Default:** Optional. At most one hit per key object or whip (whoosh, impact, buzz, wet hit), kept under the voice.
- **Range:** Inferred from the pictures only; not confirmed in the measured audio.
- **Deviate when:** Leave them out if they mask any word.

*Why:* Adds weight to the moments you see without adding words.

## Look

A clean, semi-real 3D render on uncluttered sets.

### Render
- **Default:** Semi-real 3D game-engine look: physically real or glossy materials, crisp specular highlights, fine surface detail, soft background depth of field. No outlines, toon ramps or grain.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** never

*Why:* Realism makes the facts feel documented rather than made up.

### Grade
- **Default:** A limited palette with one saturated accent reserved for the key object or the hero's clothing.
- **Range:** Bright and high-key (a warm subject against a complementary cool sky) in one reference. Dark and muted in the other, with 41–96% near-black in interiors and flat grey threat scenes.
- **Deviate when:** Use the bright complementary grade for process explainers. Use the dark muted grade for dramatic or dangerous events. Switch to an all-warm palette when the camera goes inside something.

*Why:* The accent leads the eye to what the line is about.

### Lighting
- **Default:** One clear soft key that keeps the subject readable: top or front key with sky fill, or an overhead pool with darkness around it. Self-lit displays glow.
- **Range:** Bright moderate contrast, or a low-key overhead pool
- **Deviate when:** Use flat overcast light for a threat scene before it strikes.

*Why:* The subject always reads in under a second.

### Sets
- **Default:** One uncluttered set per shot. The subject sits on a single surface or in a single space, with no extra props.
- **Range:** Minimal surface-as-landscape sets, or dense full-scale places for scale shots
- **Deviate when:** For scale, build one dense wide (crowd, city aerial). For things that happen inside, cut to a cross-section that fills the frame. For the hinge beat, use the object alone on black.

*Why:* There is nothing to read except the fact.

### Framing
- **Default:** Keep the hero on the centre vertical, within about 10% of width, upper-centre to centre. Keep the band at 70–85% of height low in detail and mid-to-dark in tone for the caption.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** For macro shots of a part, frame low at surface level looking up, so the part fills most of the frame.

*Why:* A stable centre lets the fast camera work without losing the viewer.

### Scale jumps
- **Default:** Change shot scale by at least one step at every transition (wide, medium, close), typically 3–5×, and swing between extreme wides and extreme close-ups.
- **Range:** One step up to 3–5×
- **Deviate when:** For an insert burst, keep the scale and shift the subject instead.

*Why:* Each new picture reads as new straight away.

## Characters & faces

Stand-ins and objects act through pose and action, never through faces.

### Recurring models
- **Default:** Reuse one detailed hero model in every shot it appears in. Human stand-ins have realistic proportions (about 7 heads tall) and wear one simple outfit in a single bright colour.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** A human who exists only for scale or as the one affected appears in about 1 shot.

*Why:* Viewers track the same subject without any introductions.

### No faces acting
- **Default:** No lip sync, no blinks, no expression changes. Faces are small (about 1/15 of frame height), neutral and fixed, or not shown.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** never

*Why:* Keeps the documentary distance and the diorama feel.

### Emotion through pose
- **Default:** Each figure holds one pose that tells the emotion: slumped for despair, upright for waiting, crouched for danger. A faceless object acts through what it does and through changes in its material or colour.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** never animate between poses on screen

*Why:* A pose reads instantly at any size.

### Crowds
- **Default:** Show any claim about many people with 2–3 recurring figures doing the action.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** In a pure scale wide, show the full crowd, frozen.

*Why:* A few figures make a big number feel human.

### Background figures
- **Default:** Background and crowd figures never gesture.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** never

*Why:* Keeps attention on the one action that matters.

## Motion & acting

A frozen world with short, mechanical bursts of action.

### Dead-still holds
- **Default:** Between beats, props and people don't move at all (about 0.01 px/frame of leftover motion). No idle drift, no breathing. Holds last 4–20 frames.
- **Range:** fixed (0.01 px/frame in both references)
- **Deviate when:** never

*Why:* All continuity comes from the camera, so the motion that does happen stands out.

### Action bursts
- **Default:** About 7 bursts per 10 s, each about 4 frames. Linear or ease-in motion, no anticipation, no overshoot. Hand off straight into a camera move instead of settling.
- **Range:** 5.8–7.7 per 10 s; 4–4.5 frames per burst
- **Deviate when:** A single whole-body climax gesture can take 9–11 frames with ease-in-out, then hold.

*Why:* Short, mechanical action reads as a fact, not as a performance.

### Sync to the word
- **Default:** Start an object's action 1–5 frames before its word. Place return or aftermath beats 6–13 frames after the word.
- **Range:** Leads of 1–5 frames. In one reference, a body gesture starts 3 frames after its word and reaches the key pose 8–12 frames after.
- **Deviate when:** For a repeating action, start the loop 10–12 frames before the verb that names it.

*Why:* The picture confirms the word just as it is heard.

### Repeating action
- **Default:** Alternate two parts in opposite phase, with strokes of 3–5% of frame height lasting 3–4 frames each.
- **Range:** From one reference only; stroke size estimated by eye
- **Deviate when:** Use only for actions the script calls repeated.

*Why:* Reads as continuous work without needing a long shot.

### Gesture limit
- **Default:** At most one body action per shot, and only on the climax beat.
- **Range:** 0–1 gestures per short
- **Deviate when:** When an action carries into the next shot, open that shot already in the key pose, hold 2–3 frames, then transition out.

*Why:* One gesture keeps the climax special.

### Fast elements
- **Default:** Put fast motion into particles and debris crossing at 10–20% of frame height per frame over a static set. Before an impact, keep threat elements slow and hazy.
- **Range:** From one reference only; speed estimated by eye
- **Deviate when:** Skip for calm process stories.

*Why:* Gives impact energy without animating the characters.

## Camera

Always moving, one axis per shot, arriving fast.

### Always moving
- **Default:** The camera moves in about 88% of frames.
- **Range:** 85–91%
- **Deviate when:** Allow at most 1 near-still shot in about 7: about 1 s on the hinge object, or about 4 s on one explanation beat. Leave it by easing up to about 50%/s over its last 10 frames.

*Why:* The camera supplies all the life in a frozen world.

### One move per shot
- **Default:** Each shot gets one continuous move on one main axis, never reversed. The default is a push-in (about 60% of shots); use tilts or pans for the rest. Smooth spline paths only.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** Use a tilt across a surface to reveal: bring the next subject over the frame edge 8–10 frames before the transition and keep accelerating.

*Why:* One clear direction reads as purpose, not wandering.

### Cruise speed
- **Default:** Mid-shot speed about 23.5%/s (zoom about 8.8%/s, pan about 8.6%/s).
- **Range:** Medians 19–28%/s (zoom 3.1–14.5, pan 7.7–9.5); single shots 0–116%/s
- **Deviate when:** Long shots over 2 s can cruise at 25–65%/s. Inserts under 0.5 s creep at 5–8%/s. An establishing or threat wide can use one steady 40–50% zoom over 3–4 s.

*Why:* A steady glide keeps the picture moving without hurting legibility.

### Arrive fast
- **Default:** Enter each shot at about 2.4× its mid-shot speed, carrying the transition's momentum, and ease out to cruise over 6–18 frames.
- **Range:** Median 1.9–2.8× (single shots can enter at 150–300%/s)
- **Deviate when:** never start a shot from a standstill after a motion transition

*Why:* Each shot feels like it was thrown in by the last one.

### Leave smoothly
- **Default:** Leave at about 1.2× cruise. If a whip or zoom transition follows, speed up on the same axis over the last 8–20 frames so the move is already fast when the transition starts.
- **Range:** 1.0–1.3× (single shots up to 5–7× before a whip)
- **Deviate when:** For a hard cut, keep cruising to the cut.

*Why:* Momentum flows through the join.

### No shake
- **Default:** No handheld noise or simulated shake. Holds are dead still.
- **Range:** Measured shake of 0.2–0.3% of the frame per frame comes from debris and particles, not the camera.
- **Deviate when:** never

*Why:* Clean motion keeps the documentary-render feel.

### Final move
- **Default:** End on a short pull-out that slows to a full stop on the last word.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** never

*Why:* Gives the loop a resting frame.

### Focus reveal
- **Default:** Keep focus fixed, with only a soft background blur.
- **Range:** Used in one reference only
- **Deviate when:** For a reveal, open defocused and drifting, then rack sharp over about 10 frames, finishing 3–6 frames before the subject is named.

*Why:* Delays the reveal until the word arrives.

## Cuts & transitions

Shots join through motion; most joins are blur, zoom or whip.

### Join mix
- **Default:** About 3 of 4 joins are motion transitions (blur, zoom-through, whip). About 1 in 4 is a hard cut or a 1–2 frame snap move.
- **Range:** Motion 59–92%, hard cuts 8–41%
- **Deviate when:** Insert bursts can use snap cuts. Story-driven pieces can drop to one near-hard cut per 30 s.

*Why:* The video feels like one continuous camera flight.

### Blur length
- **Default:** About 6 frames of directional blur per transition (median 5.75).
- **Range:** 5–6.5 frames median; 2–9 frames within a video
- **Deviate when:** Zoom-throughs take 5–7 frames, whips 5–6, snaps 1–2.

*Why:* Long enough to feel the move, short enough to stay fast.

### Zoom-through
- **Default:** Keep the zoom direction on both sides. Push in until a dark surface fills the frame (1–2 nearly black frames), then reveal the next shot still pushing in, with radial blur on 3–7 frames.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** For a pull-out, cross-blur both shots over about 4–6 frames while both zoom out.

*Why:* Reads as going deeper into the subject.

### Whip
- **Default:** Pan or tilt at 5–9% of the frame per frame in one direction, with 5–6 frames of streak blur along that axis. Land the new subject sharp within 4 frames, ideally on a word's first frame.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** Where possible, whip between two objects in the same space so it reads as one move.

*Why:* Links cause and consequence with a single motion.

### Paired moves
- **Default:** Answer a move later with its reverse: a dive in with a pull-out, an orbit out with an orbit back. Cross from outside to inside with one continuous push through the surface.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** never break a pair without a reason

*Why:* Gives the viewer a sense of space they can map.

### Placement
- **Default:** Don't put joins on sentence or clause ends. Place them mid-sentence, starting on a function word, so the new picture is clear 0–4 frames before the noun it shows.
- **Range:** Clause-start sync 6–15%, close to chance (8–9%)
- **Deviate when:** never cut on a sentence boundary

*Why:* Picture and voice overlap, so there is never a natural place to stop.

### No plain dissolves
- **Default:** No soft cross-dissolves between unrelated shots.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** Only the blurred pull-out dissolve is allowed.

*Why:* Every join should feel like motion.

## Captions & graphics

One small fixed caption and nothing else on top of the picture.

### Caption style
- **Default:** Small white bold rounded sans with a soft dark drop shadow. No box, no heavy outline. Centred, baseline at about 78% of frame height, letters 3–4% of frame height. A 4-word line stays under about 70% of the frame width.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** never

*Why:* Readable, and it leaves the picture as the star.

### Chunks
- **Default:** 2–4 word key phrases, punctuation and hyphens removed, no highlight words.
- **Range:** Sentence case in one reference, all lowercase in the other
- **Deviate when:** Pick one case and keep it for the whole video.

*Why:* Short chunks keep pace with 196 wpm.

### Swap timing
- **Default:** Hard 1-frame swap within ±3 frames of the phrase's first word. No pop, fade or scale. Hold the current chunk through pauses and across bursts of fast cuts.
- **Range:** -2 to +3 frames
- **Deviate when:** never animate captions

*Why:* Text matches the voice without pulling attention.

### Above transitions
- **Default:** Composite captions above the transition layer so they stay sharp and fixed while the picture blurs, whips or zooms.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** never

*Why:* An anchor the eye can rest on during fast motion.

### Safe zones
- **Default:** Keep the top 15%, bottom 18% and right 12% of the frame free of text.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** never

*Why:* Clears the platform's on-screen interface.

### No overlays
- **Default:** No labels, arrows, icons, maps, lower thirds, titles or diagram overlays.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** never

*Why:* Every fact lives inside the render.

## Facts into pictures

Every fact becomes one literal picture, timed to its word.

### One fact, one shot
- **Default:** Give each mechanism or fact its own close-up of the part or object doing it, timed so it is readable at or just before its word.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** never show a fact only in the caption

*Why:* The viewer sees what they hear.

### Counts
- **Default:** When the script counts parts, fan them out and give each one an insert in a fast burst.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** For counts of people, use a dense scale wide, then 2–3 recurring figures.

*Why:* Turns a number into something you can see.

### Numbers and times
- **Default:** Put any time, score, count or rule on a self-lit display inside the scene (timer, counter, gauge), glowing digits on dark, filling 60–70% of frame width, in its own shot.
- **Range:** From one reference only
- **Deviate when:** Change a displayed value with an instant 1-frame swap about 10 frames after the narration names it, once the camera has settled.

*Why:* Numbers stay inside the world, with no overlays needed.

### Literal comparisons
- **Default:** When the script says something works like a familiar object, morph the part's material into that object with a 3-frame in-shot dissolve that starts 0–2 frames before the comparison word. Hold 15–20 frames, then dissolve back over 3 frames.
- **Range:** From one reference only
- **Deviate when:** Skip it if the script has no comparison.

*Why:* An unfamiliar part becomes a familiar tool in about half a second.

### Hidden processes
- **Default:** When the narration names something internal, make the outer surface translucent or drain it away over 2–3 frames, 3–5 frames before the verb, then animate the inside on the verb. Alternatively, push through the surface into a full-frame cross-section.
- **Range:** fixed
- **Deviate when:** Leave the inside later with the reverse move.

*Why:* Shows what can't normally be seen, without cutting away to a diagram.

### Hinge object
- **Default:** At the turning point, isolate the key object alone on pure black (about 96% of the frame) in a near-still shot of about 1.1 s.
- **Range:** From one reference only
- **Deviate when:** Use only when the story has a single deciding object.

*Why:* Marks the moment everything turns on.

### Consequence colour
- **Default:** Mark a consequence by tinting the subject to a hot emissive colour over 3 frames, 0–15 frames after the key word, and hold the glow.
- **Range:** From one reference only
- **Deviate when:** In dark-graded stories, use a sudden burst of particles or debris instead.

*Why:* Signals 'this is the result' without words.

## Checklist for a finished video

- [ ] Runtime is 27.5–31 s, with no title card or outro. Narration starts on frame 1 and the video ends within about 0.8 s of the last word.
- [ ] The hook is one sentence of 11–14 words, ends by about 5 s, and contains a concrete count. It is not a question.
- [ ] The first picture change comes at 0.9–2.5 s, and the median shot gap is 1.5–2.2 s.
- [ ] The voice runs at about 196 wpm while speaking and covers 86–89% of the runtime. Pauses are about 0.3–0.4 s, with one longer pause of up to about 0.6 s before the payoff.
- [ ] The camera moves in 85–91% of frames, with one move per shot, never reversed, and no shake.
- [ ] Every shot enters faster than its cruise speed (about 2×) and eases down. There is at most one near-still shot per about 7 shots.
- [ ] About 3 of 4 joins are blur, zoom or whip transitions of about 6 frames. None land on sentence ends.
- [ ] Props and people are dead still between bursts of about 4 frames. There is no lip sync, no blinks and no expression change.
- [ ] Every fact has its own literal shot, readable at or just before its word. There are no labels, arrows or overlay graphics.
- [ ] Captions are 2–4 word white chunks at about 78% height with hard swaps within ±3 frames, no box, no animation and no highlight. They stay sharp over transitions.
- [ ] The master is about -10 LUFS with true peak at or below -1 dBFS, and the music bed sits about 11–15 dB under the voice with no swells.
- [ ] The last shot returns toward the first composition or ends on the hook's last word, so the loop works.

## Never

- toon outlines, cel shading, flat cartoon colours or film grain
- handheld or simulated camera shake
- reversing camera direction within a shot
- locked-off frames, apart from one hinge or explanation hold
- soft cross-dissolves between unrelated shots
- cutting on sentence or clause boundaries
- lip sync, blinks or facial expression changes
- idle breathing, drift or fidgeting between beats
- anticipation or overshoot on actions
- gestures on background or crowd figures
- labels, arrows, icons, maps, lower thirds or title overlays
- caption boxes, word highlights, emphasis colours, or pop, fade or scale caption animation
- questions or 'did you know' openers
- jokes, opinion words, morals, outros or calls to action
- pauses between words inside a clause
- music swells, drops or ducking
- true peak above -1 dBFS
- cluttered sets or extra props
